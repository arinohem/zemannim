# French and Provençal town names in Hebrew script: evidence

Compiled 2026-11-04. Read-only research; no project files changed.

## Sources and labels

Almost everything below comes from Sefaria (search API and text API). Sefaria texts are **modern digital transcriptions of printed editions**, so a form that is VERIFIED here was seen in that transcription, not in a manuscript or a scan. The printed edition is named each time, because a nineteenth-century printing of a medieval text can normalize the spelling. Rashi's laʿazim are quoted as Sefaria gives them in *Otzar Laʿazei Rashi* (Jerusalem 1988), which follows the printed Rashi with the editors' Old French reading. Manuscripts were not checked. Hebrew Wikisource stopped answering after a few queries because of rate limiting ("too many requests"), so it supplied almost nothing. The Kestenbaum auction pages for the Comtat maḥzorim (Amsterdam 1762–63) give their titles only in Latin transliteration and were no use. URL pattern: `https://www.sefaria.org/<Ref>`, with spaces replaced by underscores.

Labels: **VERIFIED** (seen in the text as described), **REPORTED** (a secondary source says so; not seen), **UNKNOWN** (not found), **MODERN** (modern Israeli or recent usage, given only as a contrast). Some identifications of a Hebrew name with a town are mine and are marked as such.

Periods: **MED** = medieval (Rashi, Tosafists, Provençal rabbis, 11th–14th c.); **EM** = 16th–19th c. (Sephardi travellers, Metz, Alsace, Paris); **MOD** = 20th–21st c.

## 1. Silent final consonants (Paris, Troyes, Sens, Reims, Orléans, Louis)

The northern medieval forms write the final -s, which was still pronounced in Old French, and write it as **ש**. Where the s followed n, they write **ץ**.

- Paris, MED: **פרי"ש** (VERIFIED). *Kol Bo* 117:2 (Venice 1547 printing), in Rabbenu Tam's takkanot: "ורבותינו שבפרי"ש". https://www.sefaria.org/Kol_Bo.117.2. Also **פריש**: "רבינו יחיאל בר יוסף מעיר פריש" in *Teshuvot ha-Rashba* VII 438 (Warsaw 1868), https://www.sefaria.org/Teshuvot_haRashba_part_VII.438.1; "ר' יחיאל מפריש" in *Seder ha-Dorot* (Warsaw 1878–82) and elsewhere.
- Paris, get formulary of 5018 (1257/8) in *SeMaG*, Positive Commandments 50 (Munkács 1901 printing): **בפרי"ש** and **בפאריש** in the same text, "מתא דיתבא על נהרי **שיינא** ו**בייברא**" (Seine, Bièvre) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Mitzvot_Gadol,_Positive_Commandments_50.9. The alef may come from the 1901 printing.
- Paris, EM: **פאריש** in an 1826 deposition ("במתא פאריש"), quoted in *Melammed le-Hoʿil* III 45 (Frankfurt 1926–32) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Melammed_Lehoil_Part_III.45.7. Also **פריש** in Shadal, introduction to Isaiah (Padua 1867) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Shadal_on_Isaiah,_Introduction.16
- Paris, EM: **פאריז**. Ḥida's circle, as quoted in Krengel's *Shem ha-Gedolim* biography: "בעיר פאריז", "מרשל די פאריז" (VERIFIED in Hebrew Wikisource, in Krengel's text). The same form appears in *Shem ha-Gedolim* / *Kuntres Peleitat Sefarim* (Podgórze 1905 edition), "נדפס מכ"י בעיר פאריז", https://www.sefaria.org/Kuntres_Peleitat_Sefarim,_Letter_Mem.78.1, and in *Teshuvot Maharsham* (1902–10) and *Or Yisrael* (Vilna 1900).
- Paris, EM: **פאריס** in *Binyan Ṣiyyon* 76 (Altona 1868): "בק"ק פאריס" (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Binyan_Tziyon.76.1. Also in Hoffmann, *Melammed le-Hoʿil* III 31 and 33.
- Paris, MODERN: **פריז** (B'Mareh ha-Bazak; VERIFIED as modern usage).
- Troyes, MED: **טרויי"ש** (*Kol Bo* 117:2, as above), **טרוייש** (*Seder Troyes*, Frankfurt 1905 edition, Introduction 1: "מעיר הקדש טרוייש"; *Teshuvot ha-Rosh* 54:1, Vilna 1881) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Seder_Troyes,_Introduction.1. *Seder ha-Dorot* also has **טריווש** ("רש"י ... מטריווש שבצרפת") (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Seder_HaDorot,_Compositions.2113.1
- Reims (my identification; it is the standard one): **ריי"ש** in *Kol Bo* 117:2, "וחכמי גבול ריי"ש" (VERIFIED). Rashi's laʿaz **ריימ"ש** (Ezek. 27:6) means *reims* "oars", not the town, but it shows the same spelling habit.
- Sens: **שאנץ** (in "ר"ש משאנץ", "תוספות שאנץ"; hundreds of hits) and **שנ"ץ** ("תוספות שנ"ץ") (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Seder_HaDorot,_Almanac,_Index.2465.1. The -ns cluster is written ץ.
- Orléans: **אורליינש** (*Paʿaneaḥ Raza*, Warsaw 1932) and **אורלינ"ש** (Maharam Schiff on Ḥullin 43a) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Paaneach_Raza,_Bechukotai.14
- Modern personal names, EM (Paris rabbinate, 19th c.; quoted by Hoffmann, *Melammed le-Hoʿil* III 31, https://www.sefaria.org/Melammed_Lehoil_Part_III.31): R. Mendel Cargau (Paris) writes **שארלאט** (Charlotte) and **לואיזע** (Louise). Hoffmann and the rabbis who agreed with him write **לואי** (Louis) without the s. R. Isaac Dov Bamberger argues that **לוי** should have no final ס, because a written ס would be read aloud. The principle, stated explicitly: a letter not heard is not written ("אות שאינה נרגשת במבטא אין כותבין כלל", for example not יאקועז for *Jacques*) (VERIFIED).

Pattern: medieval forms write the final consonant that was still pronounced (Troyes טרוייש, Sens שאנץ, Paris פריש). From the nineteenth century, French Jewish practice in gittin and ketubbot writes only what is heard (לואי, שארלאט). Paris itself kept its final consonant in every period: פריש/פאריש/פאריז/פאריס.

## 2. ch /ʃ/ (and Old French /tʃ/)

- Rashi's laʿazim render Old French *ch* (then /tʃ/) as **ק** throughout the printed text: קיברונ"ש *chevrons*, קיביל"א *cheville*, פלנק"א *planche*, פורק"א *porche*, קמבר"א *chambre*, קרדונ"ש *chardons* (VERIFIED in *Otzar Laʿazei Rashi*, e.g. Bava Batra and Bava Meṣiʿa entries). https://www.sefaria.org/Otzar_La'azei_Rashi,_Talmud,_Bava_Batra. Champagne is **קנפני"א** in Rashi on Jer. 12:10 and 31:40; the vocalized Sefaria edition has קַנְפַנְיָ"א (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Jeremiah.12.10
- Chinon, MED: **קינון** in "ר' יעקב מקינון" (*Seder ha-Dorot*; Beʾer ha-Golah; Biʾur Halakha) (VERIFIED, but in late printings). https://www.sefaria.org/Seder_HaDorot,_Almanac,_Index.1180.1
- Modern /ʃ/, EM: **ש** (שארלאט = Charlotte, R. Mendel Cargau, Paris, quoted in *Melammed le-Hoʿil* III 31) (VERIFIED). Hoffmann says "אות ש' דומה לאות Ch בלשון צרפת", meaning that Hebrew ש is the letter that matches French *ch*.

Pattern: medieval ch → ק (archaic, reflecting Old French /tʃ/ as heard or traditionally spelled); modern /ʃ/ → ש.

## 3. j and soft g /ʒ/

- Rashi, MED: Old French *j* and *ge/gi* (then /dʒ/) are written **י** (often doubled word-initially): יינציבי"ש *jencives*, ייני"ש *jeines*, יונ"ק *jonc*, יומילי"ש *jumeles*, גרני"א *granje*, לויי"ש *loges*. Sometimes **ג**: מישג"א *mesge* (VERIFIED in *Otzar Laʿazei Rashi*). Hoffmann noticed the same thing ("ברש"י ראיתי תחת J ותחת ge אות י'"), citing רוש *rouge* (Rashi on Gen. 30:32) and יונקו *jonc* (Ex. 2:2). He suggests the sound was then close to י.
- France and Alsace, EM: **ש**. R. Shemuel Ḥayyim Schuler of Bollwiller (Alsace) reports that old ketubbot written by qualified rabbis have **שאנעט** and **שאנעטטע** for *Jeanette*. Hoffmann and his bet din wrote **שאן** for *Jean*, and R. Azriel Hildesheimer (1891) agreed: שאנעטטע (Jeanette), שאן (John). R. Isaac Dov Bamberger (Würzburg, 1855) also wrote שאנעט (VERIFIED, *Melammed le-Hoʿil* III 31, paragraphs 6, 11, 24, 31, 34). In 1911 Hoffmann's bet din switched to **זש**, writing זשענני for *Jenny*, after the Russian and Hungarian practice (VERIFIED, same responsum, paragraphs 32 and 37). The Bucharest rabbi had demanded **זשאן**.
- No town name with /ʒ/ was found (Dijon, Angers, Limoges, Jarnac: UNKNOWN).

Pattern: medieval j/g → י (Rashi); traditional French and Alsatian practice → ש (שאן, שאנעט); זש is an Eastern European import adopted in Germany only in 1911.

## 4. French u /y/

- Rashi, MED: **ו**: אישקומ"א *escume*, בוצ"א *buce*, יומילי"ש *jumeles* (VERIFIED, *Otzar Laʿazei Rashi*).
- Lunel, MED and later: **לוניל** (*Minḥat Qenaʾoth*, Pressburg 1838; *Iggerot ha-Rambam*, 1859; *Seder ha-Dorot*) and **לונ"ל** (Beit Shemuel) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Minchat_Kenaot,_Correspondence.23.7. Here ו is u (Occitan /y/).
- Toulouse: **טולוזא** and **טולוזה** (VERIFIED). טולוזה is in *Seder ha-Dorot*, Almanac 5135 (Warsaw 1878–82), https://www.sefaria.org/Seder_HaDorot,_Almanac.5135.3; טולוזא is in a modern bibliography (Sefer Puʿah). **טולושא** occurs in Vital's *Shaʿar ha-Gilgulim* 36:14 (VERIFIED). That source is kabbalistic and is given only as evidence of spelling.

Pattern: u /y/ → ו, the same letter as o/ou; nothing marks the front vowel.

## 5. ou /u/

- Toulouse **טולוזא/טולוזה**; Lunel and Bordeaux, see §4 and §9. No case was found where ou is written other than **ו**. Ramerupt **רמרו"ג** (*ha-Maʾor ha-Gadol* on Ketubbot 17b, Vilna ed.; "ה"ר יעקב מרמרו"ג") (VERIFIED) has ו for -u-. https://www.sefaria.org/HaMaor_HaGadol_on_Ketubot.17b.1

Pattern: ou → ו.

## 6. oi /wa/ (Old French /oi/, /ue/)

- Rashi: **וי**: קוי"ן *coin*, פוישו"ן *foison*, אלידוי"ר *aledoir*, פושויר"ש *fossoirs* (VERIFIED, *Otzar Laʿazei Rashi*).
- Troyes **טרוייש/טרויי"ש** has the same וי (the OF diphthong of *Troies*).
- Modern /wa/ in names: **לואי** (Louis), **לואיזע** (Louise) (VERIFIED, *Melammed le-Hoʿil* III 31). These are written as heard, with וא, not as spelled.
- No modern town with oi (Blois, Foix, Choisy, Troyes in modern pronunciation) was found in a Hebrew source: UNKNOWN.

Pattern: medieval oi → וי (spelling and old pronunciation alike); for modern /wa/ the only evidence is personal names, which have וא (לואי).

## 7. eu

- Rashi: **יאו** in שיאו"ר *seur* (VERIFIED, Pesaḥim 56a entry); ליב"ש for *-levs/-leus* (קינליב"ש).
- Eugène, EM: **אייגען** (Hildesheimer 1891, in *Melammed le-Hoʿil* III 31) (VERIFIED). This is a German-style rendering (eu read as /oi/).
- Évreux, MED: **איוורא**, **איברא**, **אייברא** ("ה"ר שמואל מאיוורא", Tosafot Nedarim 80b, Vilna ed.; *Seder ha-Dorot*) (VERIFIED; identification standard). https://www.sefaria.org/Tosafot_on_Nedarim.80b.5.1. The final -eux of the modern name is absent: the Hebrew reflects a Latin- or Old French-type *Evr(e)a*.

Pattern: no stable rule; the evidence is thin and inconsistent (יאו, ייגע, or a quite different old form).

## 8. Nasal vowels (an, en, in, on)

- **Nasal written with נ**: Sens **שאנץ** (en → אנ), Champagne **קנפני"א** (*chanpanie*: an → נ before p), Dampierre **דנפירא**, **מדנפיר** (am → נ) (VERIFIED: Maḥzor Vitry 255:1, Berlin 1893–97, "רבינו יצחק מדנפירא", https://www.sefaria.org/Machzor_Vitry,_Laws_of_Purim.255.1; *Shibbolei ha-Leqeṭ*, Hilkhot Tefillin 4, Buber ed. 1886, "מדנפיר"). A variant **מדנפדרא** is reported by *Seder ha-Dorot* (VERIFIED as text). Orléans **אורליינש** (-ans → -ינש). Avignon **אויניון**, Lyon **ליאו"ן** (on → ון). Chinon **קינון**. Nancy, EM: **נאנסי** (*Nodaʿ bi-Yehuda* II, Even ha-ʿEzer 27, Warsaw 1880 printing; "אב"ד דק"ק נאנסי ומדינת לוטרינגען") (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Noda_BiYehudah_II,_Even_HaEzer.27.2
- Montpellier: **מונפשליר / מונטפשליר** (on → ון).
- Jean is **שאן**, and Hoffmann notes explicitly that the final n is written because the man said "schan" and not "schang".
- Rashi: חנכין → יינציבי"ש *jencives*, צבותים → יבילי"ש, and *-on* endings in קיברונ"ש, אישקילונ"ש: always the vowel plus נ.

Pattern: nasal vowel → vowel letter + נ (or ן at the end); m before p written נ (דנפירא, קנפניא); final -ns written ץ in Sens.

## 9. ill / ille, -eil (Marseille, Corbeil, Versailles)

- Marseille, MED: **מרשילייא** (al-Ḥarizi's preface to Maimonides' Introduction to the Mishna, Vilna ed.) and **מרשיליא** and **מרשילייא** in the same responsum (*Teshuvot ha-Rashba* IV 197, St Petersburg 1883) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Teshuvot_haRashba_part_IV.197.2. **מרשילי"א** occurs in *Iggerot ha-Rambam* (1859) (VERIFIED). **מרסיליא** occurs in *Minḥat Qenaʾoth* 97 (Pressburg 1838) (VERIFIED). These reflect Occitan *Marselha* /marˈseʎa/: -lh- → לי/ליי, final -a → א.
- Marseille, MODERN: **מרסיי** (Sacks, *Lessons in Leadership*, Hebrew ed.) (VERIFIED as modern).
- Corbeil, MED: **קורביל** ("ר' יעקב מקורביל", *Seder ha-Dorot*) (VERIFIED): -eil → יל.
- Versailles, EM: **ווירזייל** (Krengel's biography of Ḥida; *Hagahot Menaḥem Ṣiyyon*, Podgórze 1905) (VERIFIED): -ailles → ייל, intervocalic s → ז.
- Rashi: מייל"א *maille*, קיביל"א *cheville*, ארזיל"א *arzille*: ill → יל / ייל (VERIFIED).

Pattern: ill/eil → יל or ייל (Old French and modern alike); Occitan -lha in Marseille → ליא/לייא.

## 10. gn /ɲ/ (Avignon, Champagne, Perpignan)

- Avignon: **אויניון** (*Teshuvot ha-Rashba* IV 24, 89, 186, 274, St Petersburg 1883; Ibn Yaḥya on Daniel 11:36, 16th c.: "ומחוז אויניון") (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Teshuvot_haRashba_part_IV.24.1. Here gn → ני, and the v after a vowel is ו, not ב.
- Avignon, later: **אביניון** in Krengel's biography of Ḥida (Hebrew Wikisource, "בעיר אביניון (צרפת)", "בק"ק אביניון") and in a modern halakhic book (*Tenai be-Nissuin u-ve-Geṭ*, 2008) (VERIFIED as 19th–20th c. text; I did not see Ḥida's own spelling).
- Perpignan, MED: **פרפיניאן** (*Teshuvot ha-Rivash* 266 and 379, Vilna 1879) and **פרפיאן** (*Minḥat Qenaʾoth* 97 and 100) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Teshuvot_HaRivash.379.3
- Champagne: **קנפני"א**; Rashi קשטיניי"ש *chastaignes*, ציגוני"א *cigogne*, טייני"א *teigne* (VERIFIED).

Pattern: gn → ני or ניי, exactly as in the owner's Italian rule.

## 11. c before e/i, ç, s-sounds in general (Coucy, Nancy, Provence)

- Coucy, MED: **קוצי** ("ר' שמשון מקוצי"; hundreds of hits in Tosafist literature, e.g. *Seder ha-Dorot*) (VERIFIED). Old French c = /ts/ → צ.
- Provence, MED: **פרובינצא**, **פרובינציא**, **פרובינצה** (Meiri, *Minḥat Qenaʾoth*, *Shev Shemaʿtata*) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Meiri_on_Bava_Kamma.52b.5
- Rashi: צירק"א *cerche*, צירי"א *cerje* (VERIFIED).
- Nancy, EM: **נאנסי** with ס (18th c., Nodaʿ bi-Yehuda) (VERIFIED). Modern /s/ → ס.
- Marseille, MED: s → **ש** (מרשיליא); later (1838) **ס** (מרסיליא). Montpellier: s → **ש** (מונפשליר, from *Montpeslier*). Medieval /s/ is generally ש: Sens שאנץ, Paris פריש, Troyes טרוייש.

Pattern: medieval c(e/i) → צ (Old French /ts/), medieval s → ש; from the eighteenth century, French /s/ → ס (נאנסי, פאריס).

## 12. Intervocalic s /z/

- Falaise, MED: **פלייזא** ("ה"ר שמואל מפלייזא", *Paʿaneaḥ Raza*, Warsaw 1932; *Teshuvot Maharam*, Lemberg 1860) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Paaneach_Raza,_Yitro.45
- Toulouse: **טולוזא/טולוזה** (VERIFIED).
- Versailles **ווירזייל**, Paris **פאריז** (final s, voiced in liaison, or perhaps reflecting Italian *Parigi* / Ladino forms; Ḥida's usage) (VERIFIED).
- Louise **לואיזע** (R. Mendel Cargau) (VERIFIED).
- Contrast: Rashi writes intervocalic s with **ש** even where Old French had /z/: אושי"ר *user*, פוישו"ן *foison*, רויש"א *roise* (VERIFIED).

Pattern: from the Tosafist period onwards in toponyms, and always in the modern period, intervocalic s → ז, matching the owner's Italian rule. Rashi's laʿazim are the exception (ש).

## 13. Final -e muet and final vowels

- Final unstressed -e is often written **א** in medieval forms: Narbonne **נרבונא** (*Teshuvot ha-Rashba* IV 112, 182; Darkhei Moshe) and **נרבונה** (Kol Bo 101, Venice 1547; Meiri on Giṭṭin 85b), https://www.sefaria.org/Kol_Bo.101.107. Falaise **פלייזא**; Dampierre **דנפירא**. Rashi: פלנק"א *planche*, פורק"א *porche*. These reflect Old French and Occitan final -e/-a, which was then pronounced.
- Without a final vowel letter: **מונפשליר**, **לוניל**, **קורביל**, **שאנץ**.
- Arles: **ארלי** (*Minḥat Qenaʾoth* 100: "לעיר ארלי") (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Minchat_Kenaot,_Correspondence.100.1. The final י is an Occitan -e. **ארלדי** in *Teshuvot ha-Rivash* 266–267 is probably a Catalan-area town; its identification with Arles is not verified.
- Modern, EM: Hoffmann: e muet "אינו נרגש כלל במבטא" and is not written (שאן for *Jean*). R. Isaac Dov Bamberger says no final ע in שאנעט (VERIFIED). But Alsatian/German-Jewish spelling writes final unstressed -e as **ע**: לואיזע, מאטילדע (VERIFIED, same responsum).
- Bayonne: **באיונה** (*Kuntres Peleitat Sefarim* ק 30, Podgórze 1905 ed.: "טלטול שבת בעיר באיונה", on a work by Raphael Meldola, 18th c.) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Kuntres_Peleitat_Sefarim,_Letter_Kof.30.1. Also **ביונה** in a modern book (*Tenai be-Nissuin u-ve-Geṭ*).

Pattern: in the old forms final -e/-a is written א (or ה); in modern French-Jewish practice silent e is not written, while German-Alsatian scribes write ע.

## 14. -eaux / -au, and Bordeaux

- Bordeaux, EM: **בורדיאוס**. Ḥida, *Shem ha-Gedolim* (Podgórze 1905 ed.), Maʿarekhet Sefarim כ 75: "ראיתיו בעיר בורדיאוס". The 18th-c. introduction to *Sefer ha-Tashbeṣ* (Lemberg 1891 ed.) has "ובאתי לעיר בורדיאוס", with the Falcon family (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Shem_HaGedolim,_Maarekhet_Sefarim,_Letter_Kaf.75.1, https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaTashbetz,_Introduction.47. This reflects the Spanish/Portuguese name *Burdeos* / *Bordéus* used by the Sephardi nation of Bordeaux. It is not a sound-based rendering of French /bɔʁdo/.
- France itself, EM: **פראנסייא / פרנסייא** (*Sefer ha-Tashbeṣ* introduction; *Divrei Rivot* 337, Sudylkiv 1833) (VERIFIED). The older term is צרפת.
- No source for -eau(x) in any other town (Rochefort, Bordeaux in an Ashkenazi source, Meaux): UNKNOWN. Rashi uses -יל or -יאו for Old French *-el/-eau* (see §7).

Pattern: no native rule found for -eau; the one attested name, בורדיאוס, is Iberian.

## 15. h

- No French toponym with h was found. Modern Hebrew drops it (MODERN, not checked). Rashi's laʿazim do not write *h*: היריצו"ן *hericon* is the exception (VERIFIED, Bava Batra 4a entry).

Pattern: UNKNOWN for towns; mute h is probably not written.

## 16. Initial vowel

- Orléans **אורליינש**, Évreux **איוורא/איברא**, Arles **ארלי**, Avignon **אויניון**: the initial vowel carries א (VERIFIED, as above).
- Rashi: אישקילונ"ש *eschelons*, אישטרי"ך *estrich*, איגר"א *egre*, אורטיא"ש *orties*: initial e → אי, o → או (VERIFIED).

Pattern: initial vowel → א + vowel letter, as in the Italian rule.

## 17. v

- Initial v → ו: Vitry **ויטרי** ("ר' שמחה מויטרי"; *Maḥzor Vitry*) (VERIFIED). https://www.sefaria.org/Be'er_HaGolah_on_Shulchan_Arukh,_Yoreh_De'ah.39.22. Versailles **ווירזייל** (VERIFIED, 19th–20th c.).
- Intervocalic v: Avignon **אויניון** (ו) but **אביניון** (ב) later. Évreux **איוורא** (וו) and **איברא** (ב) side by side (VERIFIED).

Pattern: as in the Italian rule, but with the medieval Avignon form using a single ו where the Italian rule would give ב.

## 18. Other regions

## Summary of patterns

1. Initial vowel → א + vowel letter; final pronounced -a/-e → א in the old forms (נרבונא, פלייזא, דנפירא).
2. Nasal vowels → vowel + נ/ן; m before a labial also → נ (דנפירא, קנפניא).
3. gn → ני/ניי (אויניון, פרפיניאן); ill/eil → יל/ייל; Occitan lh in Marseille → לי/ליי + א.
4. u and ou → ו; medieval oi → וי; modern /wa/ → וא (לואי).
5. Intervocalic s → ז in toponyms (פלייזא, טולוזא, ווירזייל); medieval s elsewhere → ש; modern /s/ → ס.
6. c before e/i: medieval → צ (קוצי, פרובינצא); modern → ס (נאנסי).
7. ch: medieval → ק (קינון, קנפניא); modern /ʃ/ → ש.
8. j/soft g: Rashi → י; nineteenth-century French and Alsatian practice → ש (שאן, שאנעט); זש only from 1911 in Germany, after Eastern practice.
9. Silent letters: medieval forms keep the final consonant, which was still pronounced (טרוייש, שאנץ, פריש); modern French practice writes only what is heard (לואי, שאן).
