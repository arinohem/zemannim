# Moroccan town names in Hebrew script: research notes (2026-11-05)

Read-only research. Labels: VERIFIED = read in a transcription (source named) or in an editor's quotation of the document; REPORTED = secondary source; INFERRED; UNKNOWN.

## Sources actually searched
- Sefaria full-text search (helper script), about 200 queries. Sefaria holds little Moroccan rabbinic literature, so a zero count says little.
- Hebrew Wikisource: full-text search, plus a download of every book in its category "חכמי מרוקו" (תורה וחיים, בקש שלמה, ראה מעשה, מאה שערים, חפץ ה') and of כרך של רומי. These are transcriptions of printed books.
- Princeton Geniza Project (geniza.princeton.edu): transcriptions and editors' descriptions.
- moreshet-morocco.com (Nizri's book on Tafilalt, transcribed); the Kedem ketubbot sale catalogue PDF (auction 101a); Hebrew Wikipedia.
- Could not be fetched, so nothing is cited from them: hebrewbooks.org (Cloudflare challenge), nli.org.il and its ketubbot site (403), the Kedem website (403), and the Google Books API (429). Not reached: Ben-Naim's מלכי רבנן, Toledano's נר המערב, Zafrani, or any Moroccan get manual with a list of town names (שמות ערים). That last kind of source is still the main gap.

Assessment: in the sources I could read, the forms with כ predominate before the 20th century: מראכש (Geniza, medieval and 1801) and מאראכיש / מראכיש (Marrakesh rabbis, 1842). The ק forms appear only in 20th-century authors. I found no attestation of מאראקאש. The corpus is small (about 15 hits in all), so this is a tendency, not a count.

## Gaps and next steps
- The best untried source is a Moroccan get manual or a list of town and river names for gittin, together with the NLI ketubbot site (blocked here). Ketubbot give the documentary form ("כאן מתא X דיתבא על נהר Y").
- Both hebrewbooks and the NLI need a normal browser session (the owner's own browser).
