# Spanish and English town names in Hebrew script: evidence

Research done 2026-11-04, about 75 minutes. Sources searched: Sefaria search API and text API, Hebrew Wikisource (heavily rate-limited, so only a few queries succeeded), one web search for newspaper mastheads, and the English Wikipedia article on Judaeo-Spanish (pointer only). Labels: VERIFIED = I saw the form in the source text (almost always a modern digital transcription of a printed edition, as noted); REPORTED = given by a secondary source or catalogue that I did not check against the original; UNKNOWN = not found. Works of Yosef Caro surfaced in some searches (Bet Yosef, Avqath Rokhel) and were discarded.

Sefaria references below are given as Sefaria refs; the URL is `https://www.sefaria.org/<ref with spaces as underscores>`, e.g. https://www.sefaria.org/Teshuvot_HaRivash.301.1. The edition Sefaria transcribes is given in brackets.

Pattern: medieval Hebrew writes most Spanish towns from their Romance or Arabic forms, not from modern Castilian. Toledo, Zaragoza, Sevilla, and Lucena carry Arabic-mediated names (Ṭulayṭula → טוליטולה, Saraqusṭa → סרקסטה, Ishbīliya → אשביליה, al-Yussāna → אליסאנה); Girona is גירונדה from Gerunda; Córdoba is קרטבה/קרטובה with ט for d (Arabic Qurṭuba). Only towns without an Arabic form are spelled from the Romance name (ברצלונה, בורגוש, טודילה, מיורקה, ולנסיאה). Within that group: s → ש is regular (בורגוש, לישבונה, שוריא), as in Old Spanish where s was apical [ś]; c/z → צ in Barcelona (ברצלונה) but ס in Valencia (ולנסיאה); final -a is ה or א freely (טוליטולה/טוליטולא, מיורקה/מיורקא); e and i → י, o and u → ו; b/v both ב or ו (בלנסיאה/ולנסיאה); vowel clusters take א (ולנסיאה, קטלוניאה) or יי (קשטילייא). No consistent rule beyond the letter-for-sound ones; free variation is the norm.

## Part A2. Ladino (Judaeo-Spanish) in Hebrew script, by sound

Two layers. (a) 16th-century Ladino depositions embedded in Salonika and Balkan responsa: VERIFIED, Sefaria transcriptions of 19th-century reprints (the reprints may have dropped some diacritics). Main witnesses: Maharashdam, ḤM 95:2 (Salonika; Lemberg reprint 1862), https://www.sefaria.org/Responsa_Maharashdam,_Choshen_Mishpat.95.2; Maharashdam ḤM 5:1 (deposition from Bucharest); Divrei Rivoth 310:1 (testimony at Monastir, Nisan 5335 = 1575; Sudilkov reprint 1833), https://www.sefaria.org/Divrei_Rivot.310.1. (b) The standard 18th–20th-century orthography (Me'am Lo'ez, Ladino press): REPORTED only, from the English Wikipedia table (uncited; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaeo-Spanish#Orthography) and the National Library of Israel catalogue titles of newspapers. I could not reach a readable Hebrew-script text of Me'am Lo'ez or a Ladino newspaper: Sefaria holds no Ladino Me'am Lo'ez, NLI is blocked, and Wikisource was throttled.

### b / v
16th c.: ב for b and often for v, ו for v and sometimes for b, with much interchange: «אביאה» (avía), «אויאה» (avía), «וידו» (vido), «בולטו» (vuelto), «אישקריויא» (escrivía), «רישיוידו» / «רישיבידו» (recibido), «דיויר» (dever). Maharashdam ḤM 5:1 already has ב' with a mark for v: «ב'ינו» (vino). Later standard (REPORTED): ב = b, ב׳ = v.

Pattern: ב with a diacritic for v is the long-term Ladino solution; in the 16th century ב and ו alternate. Initial v as ו (וידו) is common in the earliest layer.

### c, z, s (sibilants)
16th c.: s is almost always ש: «שו» (su), «שיר» (ser), «שאקאר» (sacar), «אישקריטו» (escrito), «דישפואיש» (después), «שיגון» (según). Soft c and ç → ש or ס: «רישיבידו» (recibido), «גאנשייא» and «גנאסייאה» (ganancia), «פידאסו» and «פידאשו» (pedaço). Voiced z and voiced intervocalic s → ז: «דיזיאה» (dezía), «פלאזיר» (plazer), «קוזה» (cosa), «פיזי» (fize), «קיזי» (quise). Later standard (REPORTED): ס = s, ש = /ʃ/, ז = z, צ only in loanwords.

Pattern: voiced /z/ is ז in every layer, including intervocalic s in cosa (matches the Italian rule s → ז between vowels). Voiceless s was ש in the 16th century and ס later; Ladino never uses צ for Spanish c/z (only for Hebrew loans).

### j, g /x/ or /ʒ/
16th c.: no ח or כ for Spanish /x/ in the witnesses. Old Spanish j/g was /ʒ/ then, and it appears as ג: «מוגרדה» (mojada?), «אליוגי»; ambiguous with ch. Later standard (REPORTED): ז׳ = /ʒ/, ג׳ = /dʒ/; ח or כ only in Hebrew loans.

Pattern: no evidence of ח for Castilian jota in Hebrew-script Ladino, because Ladino kept the older /ʒ/; a ח spelling for j would be a modern Israeli convention.

### ch /tʃ/
16th c.: ג, often marked ג" or ג': «דיגו» / «דיג"ו» (dicho), «מוגו» (mucho), «נוג"י» (noche), «מוג' אג"ו» (muchacho), «פיג"ו» (fecho), «אינג"ו» (hinchó). Later standard (REPORTED): ג׳ (Wikipedia gives ג׳׳ for tʃ, ג׳ for dʒ).

Pattern: ch → ג with a diacritic, never צ׳ or טש. This differs from the Italian rule (soft c → צ).

### ll and y
16th c.: לי / ליי: «אקיליא» and «אקילייא» (aquella), «אישפולייאדור», «פאלייארון» (fallaron), «אליי» (allí). Later standard (REPORTED): יי or י after the merger of ll with y.

Pattern: ll → לי(י), i.e. l + doubled yod before a vowel.

### ñ
16th c.: נ + יי (+ א): «קונפאנ'יאה», «קונפנייאה», «קונפאנייאה» (compañía), «רינייון» (riñón), «מאנייאנ'» (mañana), «שנייורי'» (señores), «דאנייו» (daño). Later standard (REPORTED): ני before e/i, ניי before a/o/u.

Pattern: ñ → נייא/נייו, the same solution as the owner's Italian gn → ניי.

### Vowels and final -a
16th c.: e, i → י; o, u → ו; initial vowel carries א («אונה», «איל», «אין», «אי» for y/e 'and', «אישקריטו»); diphthongs written out: «קואנדו», «פואי», «בואינה», «טיירה», «קואינטה». Final -a is ה in most words («אונה», «לה», «אוטרה», «כוליא» is Hebrew), but א after a yod cluster or in some words («אקילייא», «גאנשייא», «בוטיקא», «אגוא», «מאטירייא»). Later standard (REPORTED): final -a always ה; initial vowel always א; hiatus separated by א or י. Newspaper mastheads (REPORTED from NLI catalogue titles): «איל טיימפו» (El Tiempo, Constantinople), «לה איפוקה» (La Época, Salonika): initial e → אי, ie → יי, final -a → ה.

Pattern: vowels follow exactly the owner's Italian rules, with one difference: Ladino writes final -a as ה, not א (the 16th-century witnesses show both).

### Other
f: «פיזי» (fize), «פואי» (fue): פ, later פ׳. h-: «הזיידנה» (hazienda) with ה. r/rr: single ר («פירו», perro). Doubled letters: never.

### Place names in Ladino or Sephardi usage (16th–20th c.)
- «בוקרישטי» (Bucharest), in a Ladino deposition, Maharashdam ḤM 5:1: VERIFIED.
- «מונישטיריו» / «מונישטרייו» (Monastir/Bitola), Divrei Rivoth 310:1: VERIFIED.
- «ריאו די זאנירו» (Rio de Janeiro), «קוראסאאו» (Curaçao), «פאנאמה», «קופאקאבאנה»: R. Ben-Ẓion Ouziel, Mishpetei Uziel VIII, OḤ 36:19 (Hebrew, mid-20th century, by a Sephardi author): VERIFIED. Portuguese j → ז; vowel sequences written with doubled א (קוראסאאו); final -a → ה.
- «פירריננטי ואיזבילה» (Ferdinand and Isabel), Seder ha-Doroth 5252:1 (18th-century Ashkenazi compiler): VERIFIED.

Pattern for Spanish-language names: a Ladino-style spelling of a Latin American or Spanish town would be: vowels as for Italian, final -a → ה, voiced s/z → ז, ch → ג׳, ñ → ניי, ll → יי, v → ב׳ (or ב), initial vowel with א. Note: Ladino gives no model for jota /x/ because Ladino pronounced those words differently.

## Part B. English-language town names, by town

Layers: MEDIEVAL (Anglo-Jewish, 12th–13th c.); 18th–19th c. (London Sephardi and Ashkenazi, rabbinic correspondence, Hebrew press); EARLY MODERN HEBREW (Ben-Yehuda 1900, Arlosoroff c. 1930); MODERN (Israeli, contrast only). I found no medieval forms for any town except London and England: the starrs edition (Olszowy-Schlanger) and Davis's Shetaroth are not online in readable form, and the one article I could open (Aberystwyth, «Peut-on parler de judéo-anglo-normand?») gives no Hebrew place-name forms.

### England (country, for context)
- MEDIEVAL: «ים האי שקורין אנגלטירא», Sefer ha-Terumah via Maḥzor Vitry, Laws of Shabbat 16:1: VERIFIED. «מהר"מ אנגלטירא» quoted in Ḥatam Sofer YD 342:4.
- 18th c.: «גלות ענגלאנד אשר ראש מלכותם לונדון, והיה אז בלונדרש», Seder ha-Doroth 5020:1 (Heilprin, 1769; Warsaw ed.): VERIFIED. «אנגלטעריא ובלשון אשכנז ענגלאנד», Pilpula Ḥarifta on AZ 39 (17th c.): VERIFIED.
Pattern: medieval and Sephardi forms come from French/Spanish Angleterre/Inglaterra; Ashkenazi forms from German England.

### Sydney, Melbourne, Johannesburg

Pattern (English overall): there is no traditional Jewish system for English names. Medieval Anglo-Jewry left only London (לונדריש) and England (אנגלטירא), both via French. From the 18th to the early 20th century Ashkenazi writers spell English names through German-Yiddish orthography: e → ע (מאנשעסטער), o → א (לאנדאן, נויארק, טשיקאגא), ch → ש or טש, final unstressed vowel → ע or א, and mostly from the written spelling rather than the sound (Manschester, Norbitsch). Modern Israeli spelling follows pronunciation (לסטר, מנצ'סטר, ליברפול). On th, w, and sh: sh → ש (שפילד); w → ב (נורביטש) or ו; th: no example found (Portsmouth, Plymouth not attested).
