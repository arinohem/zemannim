# German and Eastern European town names in Hebrew script: evidence

Research notes, 2026-11-04. Read-only historical research for the rules on writing non-Italian town names in Hebrew letters.

## Sources and labels

- **VERIFIED** means I saw the form myself in the cited text. Almost everything below comes from modern digital transcriptions (Sefaria, Hebrew Wikisource), not from images of the original prints; the edition Sefaria names is given in brackets. A transcription can normalize spelling, so a single attestation is weaker than a form that recurs across several texts.
- **REPORTED** means a source states the form or the practice (for example a responsum describing how others write), and I have not seen the primary witness.
- **UNKNOWN** means I looked and found nothing usable.
- Sefaria URL pattern: `https://www.sefaria.org/<Ref>` (spaces as underscores), e.g. https://www.sefaria.org/Terumat_HaDeshen,_Part_II_142. Counts ("n hits") are totals from the Sefaria exact-match search (`api/search-wrapper`, field `exact`) on 2026-11-04. They measure frequency in Sefaria's corpus, which is dominated by 19th-century Eastern European editions, so they are only a rough guide.
- Yiddish Wikipedia (yi.wikipedia.org) is used only as a witness of current standard or semi-standard Yiddish spelling. Its article titles are unpointed; pointed forms appear in some article leads.
- Not used as evidence: works of Yosef Caro, the Zohar, chabad.org, and Hebrew Wikipedia. Sefaria hits inside commentaries on the Shulḥan ʿarukh (Be'er ha-Golah, Pitḥe Teshuva, and others) appear in the search output; I cite them only where noted, as witnesses of a town spelling and never for anything normative.

## The three key texts on spelling place names in a get

These three discussions explain the reasons behind the spellings, so I summarize them before the sound-by-sound notes.

1. **R. Israel Isserlein, Pesaqim u-Khetavim 142** (Austria, mid-15th c.; Sefaria "Terumat HaDeshen, Part II 142", Warsaw 1882 ed.). VERIFIED. Asked how to write gittin in Rothenburg, Isserlein rules:
   - **-burg is written with ק**: רוטנבור"ק, although the Christian ("גלחות") spelling of the town seal has *g*. He lists the towns the speakers pronounce with final *g*, as written in the template he received: ווירצפור"ג (Würzburg), אוישפור"ג (Augsburg), שטרושפור"ג (Strasbourg), נורבער"ג (Nürnberg), רעגנשפור"ג (Regensburg), מרפור"ג (Marburg). He still writes all of them with ק, "because pure and sharp German is spoken that way".
   - **Short town names take a final א**, which he explains as a reduced final vowel: הגר"א, בזל"א (Basel), and in his opinion also אולמ"א (Ulm); "and the town ווינ"א (Vienna) is written so too". The river Tauber is טובר"א, not טויב"ר.
   - Rothenburg takes a single *n*, and other names in the same responsum are ורנק"ן (Franken, with ו for German *f*), רינו"ס (the Rhineland), and מענ"ץ (Mainz).
2. **Isserlein, Terumat ha-Deshen I 231**. VERIFIED. In Austria ח is pronounced like spirant כ. Isserlein says names of people, towns, and rivers with this sound should still be written with **כ, not ח**, so that Rhineland Jews, who pronounce ח as *h*, read them correctly. On the same reasoning, German *t* is written with **ט, not ת**, because ת could be read as a spirant.
3. **R. David Zvi Hoffmann, Melammed le-Ho'il III 29 and 33** (Frankfurt am Main, 1926–32 ed.; Hoffmann died in 1921). VERIFIED.
   - III 29: in gittin, Mannheim is written **מנהיים** (attested in the responsa *Ma'amar Mordekhai*, Brünn 1789), and **Hamburg is written המבורג without א**. Likewise every name ending in *-mann* takes מן without א (זעליגמן, קלמן). מאנהיים with א is acceptable after the fact but not to be preferred.
   - III 33, on how to write Brüssel in a get, surveys the older practice:
     - *ü* is written **וי** in the Memorbuch of Deutz (נוירענבערג, מוינשטר, ברויסל) because *ü* was once pronounced *ui*. It is written **ו** in the Nürnberg Memorbuch (נורנבערק, מונשטר, מונכן, מולהוזן). The edition of *Naḥalat Shiv'a* printed in Fürth has נורימבערג or נורינבערג.
     - "In our time the plain custom in Germany is to write **י for ü**", as in the German Bible and Mishna translations printed in Hebrew letters and in S. R. Hirsch's letters (דירפען, היטען, גרינדען). Brünn and Düsseldorf are written ברין and דיסלדארף, though דוסלדארף also occurs, e.g. on the title page of *Ma'amar Mordekhai*.
     - Double consonants: older Hebrew-letter writing used **one ס for German ss**. He cites the first edition of Mendelssohn's translation (וואסר, וויסן) and the forms ברויסל and דיסלדארף. Hoffmann himself argues for בריססעל with two ס in modern gittin, because German Jews now write German in Latin letters.
     - Unstressed final *-el/-en*: he wants **ע** for the reduced *e* (בריססעל).
     - Hungarian: French or Hungarian *j/zs* is written **זש** in Hungary (Pozsony = פזשאן), and that spelling spread to Russia and Galicia. Hungarian *ö* is written ו in some gittin (טורוק, קורוש).

## Part A: German, by sound

Medieval forms (11th–15th c.: Rabiah, Maharam of Rothenburg, Maharil and Isserlein, Rosh) are kept apart from early-modern ones (16th–19th c.: title pages, Acharonim, approbations).

### Vowels

**a.** Medieval forms often leave *a* unwritten in closed syllables: מגנצא (Mainz, Magenza), ורנקן, רינוס, המבורג. Early-modern forms vary between with and without א: האמבורג (Ḥakham Ṣevi, 55 hits) and המבורג (85); פראנקפורט (97) and פרנקפורט (55); האלברשטאט (52) and הלברשטאט (16) or הלברשטט (13); מאנהיים (1) and מנהיים (11). Some forms have א only in the first syllable: זולצבאך, קארלסרוא, אייזנשטאט. Hoffmann (III 29) prefers no א in gittin: המבורג, מנהיים.
Pattern: *a* = א in the plene style of the 18th–19th c., or nothing (the older and gittin practice). Both are well attested.

**e.** Medieval forms often leave it unwritten (ורנק"ן), but Isserlein already writes ע in רעגנשפור"ג, נורבער"ג, and מענ"ץ. Early-modern forms mostly use **ע**: לעמבערג (65), קעניגסבערג (8), בערלין (49, against 1007 for ברלין, where the *e* is unwritten), פרעשבורג or פרעסבורג (Pressburg; פרעסבורג is VERIFIED on the title page of *Minḥat Qena'ot*, Pressburg 1838), דעסויא, קעלן (Köln, *Ereṣ Ḥemda*, Vilna 1913). Unstressed final *-en*: מינכען (47) beside מינכן (298, mostly modern Israeli usage).
Pattern: **e = ע** from the 15th c. onward and regularly in the 18th–19th c. Unstressed *e* is often omitted (ברלין, מינכן, האלברשטאט).

**i / ie.** Always **י**: ברלין, ניקלשבורג, ווינא. Wien appears as **ווינא** (64; Maharam, "תשובה לבני ווינא"; Isserlein) and later **וויען** (133, the Yiddish name Vin). It is the Yiddish name, not a spelling of German *ie*.
Pattern: *i/ie* = י.

**o.** **ו** in medieval forms (רוטנבורק, קולוניא). Early-modern forms usually have **א** for *o* in many names, following Yiddish and Ashkenazi pronunciation: ווארמס (Worms, *Binyan Ṣiyyon*, Altona 1868), דיסלדארף, הנובר (Ḥakham Ṣevi). אלטונא keeps ו.
Pattern: *o* = ו (older), or א, especially in the 19th c. Yiddish-influenced style.

**u.** **ו**: המבורג, ווירצבורג, אויגשפורג, מונקאטש.

**ü.** Several treatments:
- **יו**: פיורדא (Fürth, 138 hits; title pages "דפוס פיורדא").
- **י**: פירדא (Fürth, 30; *Shem ha-Gedolim*), מינכען and מינכן (München), ווירצבורג, נירנבערג (modern), דיסלדארף. Hoffmann reports this as the current German-Jewish norm.
- **ו**: נורנבערק (Maharam, Prague ed. 983), נורבער"ג (Isserlein), מונכן and מונשטר (Nürnberg Memorbuch, REPORTED by Hoffmann).
- **וי**: Deutz Memorbuch (REPORTED by Hoffmann).

Pattern: medieval ו; early-modern and modern **י**; יו survives in a few fixed names (פיורדא).

**ö.** **ע**: קעלן (Köln), קעניגסבערג (Königsberg). Modern Israeli קלן and קניגסברג are also found. In Hungarian names, ו (REPORTED by Hoffmann).
Pattern: *ö* = ע.

**ei/ai.** **יי**: אייזנשטאט, אייזענשטאדט (Ḥatam Sofer), שפיירא (Speyer, rare; medieval שפירא is commoner), מנהיים, אופנהיים, שפייער (Maharik 102). Worms: ורמייזא / וורמייזא / ווירמייזא (from the Latin-based Wormacia/Wormaisa); medieval גרמיזא / גרמייזא (Rabbi Eleazar of Worms, "ר"א גרמיזא") and וורמשא (Rosh, responsum 42).
Pattern: *ei/ai* = יי.

**au.** **וי**: אוישפור"ג (Augsburg, Isserlein); אויגשפורג (*Perisha*, citing "דפוס אויגשפורג"); דעסויא and דעסוי (Dessau; Ḥatam Sofer has "דק"ק דעסוי"); הנוי and האנויא (Hanau, rare). Isserlein's טויב"ר for the Tauber also has וי.
Pattern: *au* = וי (the Western Yiddish reflex *ou*, written וי).

**eu/äu.** Few town-name examples. Deutz is דייטץ (Hoffmann), with יי. Mendelssohn-era Yiddish/German uses יי for *eu*. Pattern: *eu* = יי (one example; treat as tentative).

**Final -e.**
- Medieval: **א**, by Isserlein's rule for short names (ווינא, בזלא, אולמא, טוברא).
- Early-modern Hebrew forms in -א: פיורדא, שפירא, מגנצא, ורמייזא, קולוניא, אלטונא.
- 19th–20th c. German-Jewish usage: **ע** for a written German *-e*, e.g. קארלזרוהע (Hoffmann III 28) beside the older קארלסרוא (*Ma'amar Mordekhai* era; *Kuntres Peleṭath Sefarim*, "קארלסרוא תקפ"ח").

Pattern: Hebrew final א is the traditional mark of a name ending in a vowel or reduced vowel; ע is the later German-Jewish and Yiddish one.

### Consonants

**sch.** **ש**: שפירא, שטראסבורק, אויגשפורג, ניקלשבורג, האלברשטאט.

**s before p/t (/ʃ/).** **ש**: שפירא, שטרושפור"ג, אייזנשטאט, הלברשטאט, שטראסבורג.

**Medieval s generally.** Medieval forms use **ש** even for German /s/ (and /z/) in many words: אוישפור"ג and שטרושפור"ג (*-sburg*), וורמשא (Worms), and the Yiddish letter of Isserlein's wife Schöndlein preserved in *Leqeṭ Yosher* II 19 (אוש, האשט, אלש, איזט/אישט).
Pattern: medieval ש for every sibilant except צ; later ס/ז for /s/ and /z/.

**Initial s before a vowel (/z/).** Early-modern **ז**: זולצבאך (Sulzbach, 31 hits; *Seder ha-Dorot*, "נדפס זולצבאך"; Noda bi-Yehuda EH I 41, "פה זולצבאך"), זלצבורג (Salzburg, *Oṣar Midrashim*), זעליגמן (Hoffmann). I found no medieval town example.
Pattern: German /z/ = ז.

**Intervocalic s.** The medieval Worms forms have ז (ורמייזא, גרמיזא) or ש (וורמשא). Modern German /s/ between vowels is not well represented in my sample. Hoffmann (III 33) shows that German *ss* was historically written with one ס (ברויסל, דיסלדארף).

**z / tz.** **צ**: מגנצא, מענ"ץ (Isserlein), מענץ (*Qav ha-Yashar*, Frankfurt 1705), זולצבאך, ווירצבורג, דייטץ, ניקלשבורג (no z there). Final form ץ.
Pattern: German z/tz = צ (ץ word-finally) in every period.

**ch (velar/palatal).** **כ / ך**: זולצבאך, מינכען, *Qav ha-Yashar* "קהל מענץ". Isserlein (TH I 231) gives the reason to prefer כ over ח: Rhineland Jews read ח as *h*. Polish and Austrian-Jewish names sometimes have ח (חעלם for Chełm, *Seder ha-Dorot*, 7 hits, against כעלם; טשענסטחוב).
Pattern: German *ch* = כ/ך; ח appears in Slavic names.

**w.** Medieval forms often use **single ו**: ורמייזא, ורנק"ן. Early-modern and later forms use **וו**: ווירצבורג, ווינא, וויען, ווירמייזא, ווארמס, וואנזבעק (Wandsbek, *Shem ha-Gedolim*, "נדפס בעיר וואנזבעק").
Pattern: w = וו in the early-modern period.

**German v and f.** Initial *F* is פ in place names: פראנקפורט, פיורדא, פרייבורג. Medieval forms have ו or ב for *f/v*: ורנק"ן (Franken, Isserlein); בון (von) and ברויאן (Frauen) in *Leqeṭ Yosher* II 19. I found no German town in *V-* (= /f/) on title pages. UNKNOWN.
Pattern (tentative): /f/ = פ in modern names; medieval ו/ב for *f* is a Yiddish spelling habit and does not occur in modern town names.

**h.** Initial *h* = ה: המבורג, האלברשטאט, הנובר, הלברשטאט. Medial *h* after a vowel (Ruhe, Dyhernfurth) is either dropped (קארלסרוא) or written (קארלזרוהע; דיהרנפורט, 7 hits, *Shem ha-Gedolim* and *Peri Megadim*). *-heim*: מנהיים, אופנהיים (86); אופנהים (Rosh 42, medieval).

**t / d.** German *t* = ט (Isserlein's rule; האלברשטאט, ברלין has none). Final *-d* is often written ט according to the pronunciation (אייזנשטאט; אייזענשטאדט also occurs). Fürth is פיורדא with ד. Dyhernfurth is דיהרנפורט.

**-burg.** Medieval **בורק / פורק** with ק (Isserlein, Darkhe Moshe EH 128:16 quoting him: רוטנבורק, שטראסבורק, "in ק at the end, as the Ashkenazim speak"). Also פורק after *s* (*-sburg* → שפורק). Early-modern **בורג**: המבורג, ווירצבורג, פרעשבורג, ניקלשבורג, רעגנשבורג (Noda bi-Yehuda EH I 26). ניקלשפורג (8 hits, *Kunṭres Peleṭath Sefarim*) and אויגשפורג keep the older פ after ש.
Pattern: medieval -בורק; later -בורג. *-sburg* becomes -שבורג or -שפורג.

**Double letters.** Not doubled in older practice (Hoffmann: one ס for *ss*; one נ in Rothenburg; one נ in Mannheim). Hoffmann's late proposal of סס is his own innovation. Pattern: **no doubling**.

Pattern for German: by the 18th century the norm is close to Yiddish spelling: ע for *e*, יי for *ei*, וי for *au*, י for *ü*, וו for *w*, ש for *sch* and for *s* before *p/t*, ז for initial /z/, צ for *z*, כ for *ch*, ט for *t*, -בורג for *-burg*, and no doubled letters. Older or "Hebraized" names keep a final א (פיורדא, מגנצא, ורמייזא, אלטונא, ווינא). The medieval layer differs in four points: single ו for *w* (or ג in גרמיזא), ש for every *s*, -בורק with ק, and frequent omission of vowels.

Polish and other Slavic sounds, with examples from the table and the extra searches:

- **sz** = ש (פשעמישל, Yiddish).
- **cz** = טש (בארדיטשוב, טשערנאוויץ, טשענסטחוב).
- **szcz** = שטש (זאמאשטש for Zamość is Yiddish *-oshtsh*, from yi.wikipedia; Hebrew זאמושץ in *Ḥokhmat Shelomo*). I did not find a clean *szcz* town.
- **rz** = ש/ז (פשעמישל; בריסק from Brześć).
- **ż / ź** = **ז** in older Hebrew forms (זיטאמיר, וואלאזין, מעזיבוז, לאדז) and **זש** in Yiddish (זשיטאמיר, וואַלאזשין, מעזשביזש, לאָדזש). Hoffmann reports זש as the Hungarian-Jewish spelling for *zs*.
- **ł** = ל (סלוצק, ביאליסטאק, וואלאזין, לאדז).
- **w** = וו (ווארשא, ווילנא); final *-ów* is -וב in Hebrew (בארדיטשוב, פיעטרקוב) and -וו or -עוו in Yiddish.
- **ó** = א or ו (לאדז; קראקא).
- **ę / ą**: I found no clear town example. UNKNOWN.
- **Slavic h** = ה (הוראדנא). Yiddish גראדנע has ג from the Russian/Polish form.
- **ch** = ח or כ (חעלם 7 against כעלם; טשענסטחוב).
- **Hungarian**: sz = ס (סאטמאר), s = ש (מיקלאש, Ḥatam Sofer EH II 34), cs = טש (מונקאטש), zs = זש (פזשאן).

Pattern for Eastern Europe:

- **Two layers.** The Hebrew title-page and rabbinic layer (18th–early 20th c.) ends names in a final vowel with **א** (ווילנא, ווארשא, קראקא, הוראדנא, קאוונא, ריגא, אדעסא, פרעמישלא). It writes *o* with א (ביאליסטאק, וואלאזין, זיטאמיר), *e* with ע, and *ż* with plain ז.
- **Yiddish** replaces the final א with **ע** (ווילנע, וואַרשע, קראָקע, קאָוונע, ריגע, גראָדנע), writes *ż* as זש, and *-ów* as -וו. The YIVO standard adds pointing (אַ, אָ, פּ).
- **Separate Jewish names** unrelated or only distantly related to the current official name: לעמבערג/לבוב (Lviv), בריסק (Brest), הוראדנא (Grodno), קאוונא (Kaunas), ווילנא (Vilnius), סאטמאר (Satu Mare), מונקאטש (Mukachevo), טשערנאוויץ (Chernivtsi), יאס (Iași), פרעמישלא (Przemyśl, Hebrew). Most come from the Polish, German, or Hungarian name used when the Jewish community flourished, not from today's official language.

## Exception candidates

These are names whose traditional Jewish form does not follow from the general rules, so an app would need to store them individually:

1. Mainz: **מגנצא** (from *Magontia/Magenza*), not מיינץ.
2. Worms: **ורמייזא / וורמייזא** (from *Wormatia*), medieval גרמיזא.
3. Speyer: **שפירא**.
4. Köln: **קולוניא** (Latin), later קעלן.
5. Fürth: **פיורדא** (יו for *ü*, ד, final א).
6. Frankfurt: abbreviations **פפד"מ** and **פפד"א**; plain פראנקפורט or פרנקפורט.
7. Wien: **ווינא** (Hebrew) or **וויען** (Yiddish).
8. Pressburg: **פרעשבורג** (Hebrew/German) against Hungarian-based פזשאן.
9. Lviv: **לבוב** or **לעמבערג**.
10. Brest: **בריסק**.
11. Grodno: **הוראדנא**.
12. Kaunas: **קאוונא**.
13. Satu Mare: **סאטמאר**.
14. Mukachevo: **מונקאטש**.

16. Iași: **יאס**.
