# Attested Hebrew spellings of Italian towns: second search (2026-11-04)

Scope: the towns that the first search left open, plus a frequency check on Mantova and Pesaro. Forms that the first search had already found are not repeated, except where this search met them again.

Labels:
- **VERIFIED**: I saw the form myself, in a scan or in a text transcription. The source type is given.
- **REPORTED**: the form comes only from a secondary source, or from a later author quoting an earlier one.
- **UNKNOWN**: no attestation found.

"Sefaria" means Sefaria's own transcription, found through the search API (`/api/search-wrapper`, field `exact`) and read in context. Sefaria's coverage of Italian rabbinic literature is thin: there is no Paḥad Yiṣḥaq, no Ghirondi or Mortara, and no Maʿgal Ṭov. Absence from Sefaria therefore says little.

## Mantova and Pesaro: variants and frequency

Counts are Sefaria `exact` hits; each count is roughly the number of passages. Modern editors make up much of the total.

**Mantova**
- מנטובה 65, ממנטובה 23, במנטובה 25, מנטובא 10, במנטובא 2. Total about 125.
- מאנטובה 2, ממאנטובה 2, מאנטובא 1. Total 5.
- The forms with alef are rare, but they occur in Italian-connected authors:
  - Isaac Lampronti, *Paḥad Yiṣḥaq* (Ferrara), quoted in *Yein ha-Ṭov* I O.Ḥ. 8:7: «כמוהר"י בריאל ממאנטובה» (REPORTED).
  - Elia Benamozegh (Livorno), *Em la-Miqra*, Genesis 28:14: «הרב מורטארא אב"ד של ק"ק מאנטובא» (VERIFIED, transcription).
  - *Admaṯ Qodesh* Ḥ.M. 74 (Salonica): «רבני מתא דק"ק מאנטובה».
- Ḥida writes both «במנטובה» (*Shem ha-Gedolim*, Gimel 20) and «במנטובא» (ʿAyin 73).
- **Conclusion:** מנטובה is the majority form by a wide margin. מאנטובה/מאנטובא is a real Italian variant, but uncommon.

**Pesaro**
- With ס: פיסארו 6, בפיסארו 1, מפיסארו 2.
  - *Kerakh shel Romi* (I.M. Ḥazan, Rome) uses only פיסארו: «בפיסארו עיר קצה גבול מלכות האפיפיורו'»; «רומא אנקונא פיסארו פירארה» (VERIFIED, transcription).
  - *Seder ha-Doroṯ*: «ר' אהרן מפיסארו».
  - Uziel, *Mishpeṭe ʿUzziʾel* (20th century): «בעיר פיסארו».
- With ז: פיזארו 21, בפיזארו 3, מפיזארו 1, plus פיזרו in the formula «דפוס פיזרו».
  - Almost all of these are modern bibliographic usage for the Soncino press at Pesaro ("דפוס פיזארו", Eisenstein, *Mishnaṯ Ereṣ Yisraʾel*).
  - One older instance is *Avqaṯ Rokhel* §179: «שהיהודים הדרים בפיזארו».
- **Conclusion:** among Italian Jewish authors in this sample, פיסארו is the native form. פיזארו dominates only in modern bibliographic citations of the press. I did not see the Soncino colophons themselves.
